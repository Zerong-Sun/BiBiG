# Biography — A Very Short Introduction

Conceptual and critical biography skill distilled from Hermione Lee, *Biography: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford University Press, 2009). Use when the task is **what biography is**, **how to read or judge it**, or **which rules and metaphors apply** — complementing hands-on writing skills like `how-to-do-biography`.

## First Moves

1. Clarify whether the user needs **theory** (definitions, rules, metaphors), **evaluation** (judging a draft or published life), or **design** (choosing stance, coverage, ethics).
2. Pick a reference:
   - `references/ten-rules.md` — Lee's ten rules with qualifications and exceptions
   - `references/metaphors-and-definitions.md` — autopsy vs portrait; what biography is
   - `references/workflow.md` — research, structure, ethics, controversy (Chinese)

   - `references/validation.md` — decision rules, risks, quality gate (Chinese)

## Core Frames

- **Autopsy metaphor**: posthumous scrutiny; can harm survivors; limited on inner life; subjectivity remains.
- **Portrait metaphor**: empathy and presence; risk of flattery, flatness, distortion; subjects change over time unlike static portraits.
- **Working definition**: biography is the **story of a person told by someone else** — narrative, not mere fact-listing; always involves selection and shaping.

## Operating Rules

- Every biography entails **shaping and selection**; "omit nothing" is an ideal, not a practice.
- Treat testimony with scepticism; repeated untruths congeal into received versions.
- Balance involvement and detachment; biography is a "broken bridge into the past" (Richard Holmes).
- Biography has duties to **history** (the stream) as well as to **the individual** (the fish).
- Flag when a single theory (psychoanalysis, Marxism, etc.) may be simplifying the life.

## Expected Outputs

Glossary notes, rule-by-rule assessment of a project, metaphor choice with tradeoffs, reader-contract statement, ethics memo, or structured critique of an existing biography. Distinguish fact, inference, speculation, and silence.
