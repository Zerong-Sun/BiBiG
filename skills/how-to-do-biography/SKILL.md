# How To Do Biography

Practical biography primer distilled from *How To Do Biography: A Primer* by Nigel Hamilton (Harvard University Press, 2008). Use for **print biography** of real individuals — not fiction, roleplay, or invented scenes.

## First Moves

1. State the assignment: subject, scope, reader, length, deadline, sources, and whether the subject or close relations are alive.
2. Write the biography's **agenda** in one paragraph: what public question this life helps answer.
3. Name the **audience** and what the book implicitly promises (correction, rediscovery, moral inquiry, career portrait, intimate life, public life, cultural history through one person).
4. Open the right reference:
   - `references/primer-outline.md` — Hamilton's 16-chapter map (start here for full projects)
   - `references/workflow.md` — research-to-publication workflow
   - `references/checklists.md` — planning, scenes, structure, revision
   - `references/ethics-and-fact-checking.md` — truth, privacy, living people, disputed material

## Operating Rules

- Biography records and interprets a human life; clarity is essential — do not bury the reader in subsidiary cases.
- Do not invent dialogue, events, motives, or interior states. Write uncertainty into the prose when evidence is partial.
- Treat memoir, interviews, and family testimony as claims to verify.
- Prefer a **shaped life** over a chronological dump; use chronology as spine, not as the whole design.
- Research and writing proceed together once the core timeline is stable; drafting reveals missing evidence.
- Distinguish autobiography, memoir, and biography — each has different truth obligations and reader contracts.

## Expected Outputs

Produce what the user needs: agenda note, audience profile, research plan, annotated timeline, chapter architecture, scene list, draft passage, ending strategy, truth-risk register, fact-check table, or publication-ready prose. Mark confidence levels when facts are uncertain.
