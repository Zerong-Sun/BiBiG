# Hamilton Primer — 16-Chapter Map

From Nigel Hamilton, *How To Do Biography: A Primer* (2008). Use as a stage-by-stage checklist, not a rigid linear pipeline.

## Part I — Getting Started

| Ch | Topic | Agent focus |
|----|-------|-------------|
| 1 | The Task of Biography | What biography contributes to knowledge; ethics and interpretation of a human individual |
| 2 | What Is Your Agenda? | Private motive vs public question; what existing stories need confirming or overturning |
| 3 | Defining Your Audience | Scholarly, general, family, institutional readers; tone, pace, context, notes |
| 4 | Researching Your Subject | Source inventory; contemporary vs retrospective evidence; counterevidence early |
| 5 | The Shape of a Life | Turning points vs events; what to omit; thematic spine inside chronology |

## Part II — Composing a Life-Story

| Ch | Topic | Agent focus |
|----|-------|-------------|
| 6 | The Starting Point | Open after enough draft exists to know the real center of gravity |
| 7 | Birthing Your Subject | Birth only when dramatic, contested, or thematically central |
| 8 | Childhood and Youth | Include only when it illuminates later patterns, wounds, or choices |
| 9 | Love Stories | Relationships that changed direction, work, or self-concept |
| 10 | Life's Work | Public achievement tied to evidence, not hagiography |
| 11 | The Twilight Years | Avoid slow inventory of decline unless decline is the subject |
| 12 | Ending Your Story | Resolve the book's question without pretending to resolve the whole life |

## Part III — Variations on a Theme

| Ch | Topic | Agent focus |
|----|-------|-------------|
| 13 | Autobiography and Memoirs | Self-presentation as evidence; different reader contracts |
| 14 | Memoir | Narrower scope, witness memory, less total-life obligation |
| 15 | Truth—and Its Consequences | Legal, ethical, and reputational risk; attribution in prose |
| 16 | The Afterlife | Reputation, myth, suppression, posthumous reputation when it matters |

## Cross-Stage Questions

- What would make this biography necessary if another already exists?
- Which relationships changed the subject's choices?
- Does this structure reveal causality, or only sequence?
- Where does the subject's own account conflict with other evidence?
- Is the conclusion the natural result of evidence, or the writer's prior agenda?
