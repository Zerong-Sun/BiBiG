# Biography Checklists

## Project Definition

- Subject, scope, and time span are explicit.
- The biography's public question is stated in one paragraph.
- Target reader is named, with implications for jargon, notes, pace, and context.
- The project distinguishes admiration, grievance, curiosity, and public value.
- Existing biographies or dominant narratives have been surveyed.

## Research

- Dates, places, and major relationships have source trails.
- First-hand, second-hand, retrospective, hostile, anonymous, and family sources are labeled.
- Memoir and interview claims are treated as claims until corroborated.
- Counterevidence has been actively searched for and logged.
- Each archival citation includes enough locator detail to find it again.
- Source gaps are visible rather than hidden.
- Witness interviews are compared against documents and against one another.
- Quoted material is checked against the original, not copied from another writer when avoidable.

## Timeline and Causality

- Timeline distinguishes ordinary events from turning points.
- Cause and effect are shown through evidence, not assumed from sequence.
- Childhood material is included only when it illuminates later patterns or wounds.
- Public work and private life are connected when the evidence supports a connection.
- Late-life chapters avoid becoming a slow inventory of decline unless decline is the subject.

## Structure

- The opening establishes voice, stakes, and the central question.
- The structure uses chronology as a spine but allows thematic shaping.
- Each chapter has a dramatic question or problem.
- Chapter endings create consequence, pressure, or transition.
- The ending resolves the book's question without pretending to resolve the whole life.
- The afterlife of the subject is included when reputation, legacy, myth, or suppression matters.

## Scenes

- Scene details are supported by evidence: place, time, participants, action, and sensory context.
- Dialogue is quoted only from reliable records or clearly attributed recollection.
- Inner life is inferred cautiously and signposted when uncertain.
- Every scene advances plot, reveals character, deepens theme, or clarifies context.
- Vividness does not come at the cost of truth.

## Relationships

- Important relationships are represented from more than one viewpoint where possible.
- Love, sex, marriage, rivalry, family, patronage, and betrayal are tied to the life story's meaning.
- Private relationships are not used merely as spectacle.
- Living relatives and associates receive heightened care and evidence thresholds.
- Power dynamics are described concretely rather than moralized abstractly.

## Prose and Revision

- Unsupported adjectives and adverbs have been replaced with facts or removed.
- The prose does not flatten the subject into a hero, villain, diagnosis, or case study.
- Claims of motive use evidence and appropriate uncertainty.
- The draft makes room for contradiction without becoming evasive.
- Repeated exposition has been cut or moved.
- Notes, captions, acknowledgments, and author's note match the evidentiary method.

## Final Risk Pass

- Sensitive claims have evidence grades.
- Potentially defamatory claims have multiple reliable sources or are softened/removed.
- Privacy intrusions have a written necessity rationale.
- Copyright permissions and fair-use risk have been reviewed.
- Disputed evidence is acknowledged in text, notes, or author's note.
