# Biography Writer

Use this skill for nonfiction life writing about real people. Do not use it for purely fictional lives, historical roleplay, or invented scenes.

## First Moves

1. Clarify the assignment: subject, scope, intended reader, length, deadline, available sources, and whether the subject or close relations are alive.
2. State the biography's working agenda in one paragraph: what public question this life helps answer.
3. Build a provisional life timeline and mark the likely turning points, source gaps, living witnesses, and sensitive zones.
4. Choose the right reference file:
   - Read `references/workflow.md` for full projects, proposals, outlines, or stuck drafts.
   - Read `references/checklists.md` when planning research, scenes, structure, relationships, or revision.
   - Read `references/ethics-and-fact-checking.md` before using private, disputed, defamatory, anonymous, or single-source material.

## Operating Rules

- Do not invent dialogue, events, motives, or interior states. If the evidence is partial, write the uncertainty into the prose.
- Treat autobiography, memoir, interviews, and family testimony as claims to verify, not as final fact.
- Keep asking whether a detail is necessary to understand the subject's character, work, decisions, relationships, or historical effect.
- Prefer a shaped life story over a chronological dump. Use chronology as a spine, not as the whole design.
- Keep a source trail for every important assertion, especially dates, places, relationships, accusations, medical details, finances, sex, politics, and causes of death.
- When writing about living people, raise the evidentiary threshold and reduce unnecessary harm.

## Expected Outputs

When asked to help, produce the artifact the user needs: a research plan, annotated timeline, chapter architecture, scene list, draft passage, revision notes, risk register, fact-check table, or publication-ready prose. Include confidence levels or source needs when facts are uncertain.
