# Footsteps — Romantic Biographer

Field biography skill distilled from Richard Holmes, *Footsteps: Adventures of a Romantic Biographer* (Hodder, 1985). Use when biography requires **going to places**, **following physical trails**, and **writing the pursuit** — not only desk archives. Complements `how-to-do-biography` (primer) and `biography-vsi` (theory).

## First Moves

1. Name the **core question** — the fundamental tension or mystery that will thread the life (Holmes on Shelley, Stevenson, Nerval).
2. Decide whether the project needs **footsteps work**: maps, routes, landscapes, re-readings on site, local witnesses.
3. State the **broken-bridge stance**: involved pursuit across an unattainable past; never pretend to fully "catch" the subject.
4. Open references:
   - `references/broken-bridge.md` — vocation, metaphor, pursuit ethics
   - `references/workflow.md` — field → archive → inner journey → narrative (Chinese)
   - `references/checklists.md` — field, archive, cross-validation, writing (Chinese)
   - `references/validation.md` — evidence rules, speculation, risks (Chinese)
   - `references/ethics-and-fact-checking.md` — if present; else use validation.md

## Operating Rules

- Biography as **pursuit**: tracking the physical trail of someone's path through the past; writing the chase may bring the figure alive in the present.
- **You will never quite catch them** — honesty about limits is part of the method.
- **Place is evidence**: weather, terrain, distances, what the subject saw from a window; sensory notes at key nodes.
- Keep a **parallel field diary**: your reactions are indicators for calibrated empathy, not facts about the subject.
- **Text archaeology**: compare published, draft, and private versions; find what was toned down, encrypted, or erased.
- Distinguish **social self vs private self** — gaps between autobiographical publication and field truth.
- No invented dialogue; mark inference; external evidence before recording inner states.
